\section{Bridge from mismatch discovery to mediation}
\label{sec-app-bridge}

An early motivating question was whether a sparse feature pair could repair
a grammatical mismatch while preserving the planned noun token. On a source
prompt of the form ``Someone who treats eye diseases is,'' suppressing a
competing feature pair crossed the decision boundary from majority
\texttt{a} toward \texttt{an} while preserving \texttt{ophthalmologist}
on that prompt. The held-out transfer of the same frozen pair did
\emph{not} behave as a noun-preserving grammar tool: many expected-\texttt{a}
controls flipped toward \texttt{an}, and noun tokens changed on 37/107
prompts, with zero grammar-only repairs that kept the noun fixed
(machine-readable results in \texttt{fixed\_pair\_generalization/}).

That failure sharpened the question from ``can we fix articles?'' to
``do Latent-Planning-style dual-effect interventions identify separable
noun-token control, or can free-generation noun changes be explained by
mediation through the generated article?'' Behavioral article-recall
baselines on this 270M stack strongly prefer majority \texttt{a}; reliable
\texttt{an} when required by listed targets is rare---consistent with
\citet{hanna2026latent}'s scale story. Subsequent main-text assays address
that sharpened question.

\section{Additional selection details}
\label{app:selection}

S1--S3 are selected from intersection of article and future-content
attribution graphs at position \(P\), with article direct-effect bounds for
S3. Per-noun assays select top-4 features per family by target direct
effect (and by target--source contrast) subject to
\(|\mathrm{DE}(a/an)|\le 0.05\). Residual assays unit-normalize
per-layer MLP-in differences before scaling by \(\alpha\). Oracle
positive-control patches blend source and target MLP-in activations at
layers \(\{8,10,12,14\}\) under fixed native \(b\).

The six-cell runs reuse the S1--S4 sets and S1 random-control features
that were fixed from selection-occupation attribution graphs before those
runs. They cross each handle off/on with free, inserted-\texttt{a}, and
inserted-\texttt{an} articles. The original S1 \(5\times\) factorial also
ran frozen-attention semantics. Primary intervals are prompt-level
nonparametric bootstrap intervals with 10,000 resamples.

\paragraph{Exploratory free-generation ablations.}
Across S1--S4 at \(5\times\), wrapper-like rate is \(0/20\); only S1
produces package-like switches at 0.95. Across S1 doses
\(\{1.5,2,3,5,8\}\times\), package-like rate rises from 0.10 to 0.95 while
wrapper-like rate remains zero. Adding an S3 content lock does not prevent
S1 from selecting legal \texttt{an}+vowel-initial completions. These
results motivate decomposition of S1 but are not the primary mediation
evidence.

\section{Factorial, propagation, and residual-reference details}
\label{app:factorial-details}

On the 19 prompts where S1 changes the article, recomputed-attention mean
TV is 0.9397 for the total contrast, 0.9374 for article insertion alone,
and 0.0288 for the same-prefix residual. The prompt-bootstrap 95\%
interval for residual/total is [0.023, 0.040]. Frozen-attention values are
0.9393, 0.9374, and 0.0269, with ratio interval [0.022, 0.037].
The all-20 analysis is primary; the switch-conditioned analysis is
descriptive.

For the propagation diagnostic, S1 is applied at the pre-article position
and \texttt{an} is pasted. At the article position, recomputed-attention
mean sparse-activation \(\Delta L_2\) rises from zero through layer 10 to
11.6 at layer 11, 133.8 at layer 12, and remains nonzero through layer 17.
The corresponding mean noun-distribution TV is 0.0252. Frozen attention
also propagates the intervention (mean TV 0.0328).

The full-residual reference uses paired source/target vocabulary cues with
an identical occupation sentence and fixed \texttt{a}. Layers are swept on
four development pairs; layer 12 is selected. On four disjoint test pairs,
the target residual yields mean target-specific
\(\Delta\Delta=6.44\,[4.19,10.06]\), while ten matched-norm random
directions averaged within pair yield \(0.14\,[-0.33,0.61]\).

Equality of free S1-on noun logits with S1-on logits after inserting the
treated article is an implementation consistency check (maximum absolute
error zero in the original factorial). It is not a scientific result.

\paragraph{Identification argument.}
Let \(Y(i,b)\) be the noun distribution under intervention \(i\) and
article \(b\). The free-generation contrast is
\(E_T=Y(1,B_1)-Y(0,B_0)\). If \(Y\) depends on \(B\) only through the
visible article token, then \(Y(1,b)=Y(0,b)\) for each inserted \(b\), so
\(E_R=0\) and \(E_T=E_M\). If a leftover internal effect remains after
\(B\) is fixed, \(E_R\neq 0\). Observing \(E_T\) alone cannot distinguish
those cases. Cosine and TV of \(E_M\) versus \(E_R\) are descriptive
summaries of those vectors; they are not a percent-mediated.

\paragraph{Family sweep.}
Shared intervention-off insertion of \texttt{an} versus \texttt{a} yields
noun TV \(0.937\,[0.891,0.975]\) on the 20 held-out prompts. A
transformers-only baseline with no sparse intervention yields
\(0.940\,[0.893,0.976]\) on those prompts and
\(0.975\,[0.964,0.983]\) on 95 released \texttt{an}-target occupations,
with top-1 noun change on every prompt. S1 at \(5\times\) has
\(\operatorname{cosine}(E_M,E_T)=0.949\,[0.849,1.000]\) and reconstruction
L1 numerically zero. S2/S4 ``noun changed'' rates often reflect greedy
strings that are not a legal article--noun pair. S2's twin leftover under
\texttt{an} (\(+0.830\)) is not interpreted as a content plan.

\section{Synthetic estimand-validation details}
\label{app:synthetic}

The synthetic vocabulary contains a cue marker, articles \{\texttt{a},
\texttt{an}\}, four filler tokens, and eight source/target noun pairs.
Both mechanisms share article-bias and noun--article coupling parameters;
only the direct mechanism adds a persistent plan bias to noun logits after
the article is fixed. Under the mediated mechanism, mean total and
article-only TV are both 0.905, residual TV is 0, and both force-\texttt{a}
and force-\texttt{an} residual-control curves are identically zero for
\(k\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). Under the direct mechanism, force-\texttt{a} CDE TV
is 0.667 with target-minus-source \(\Delta\Delta=6.25\), and the
force-\texttt{a} curve remains 0.667 for every \(k\). Force-\texttt{an}
residual TV is 0.090 with the same logit \(\Delta\Delta\), illustrating
saturation of distributional residuals when the mediator already favors the
target. Machine-readable outputs live in
\texttt{synthetic\_token\_mediation/}.
