# Couplet routes: report

_Generated by `make_report.py` from the committed JSON in `results/`. Design and commands: `../README.md`. Terms: `docs/glossary/route-accounting-glossary.html`._

## Motivation

Rhyming couplets are the flagship example of apparent latent planning (Lindsey et al. 2025; Hanna & Ameisen 2026): the rhyme word of line 2 seems to be chosen in advance, at the end of line 1. The question here is how that choice reaches the end of line 2, about 16 positions later: by **emission** (through the line-2 words the model writes), by **direct retrieval** (the rhyme position reads the end of line 1 back), or by **relay** (carried forward privately through the positions in between). Relay is the novel possibility; retrieval and emission are the conservative ones.

## What was measured

Hanna & Ameisen's 100-couplet steering subset per model, their prompt, their anchor (the last word of line 1) and their donor pairing (a couplet with a different rhyme). The edit places the donor's anchor state at the anchor (all layers, text unchanged; **state edit**) or applies the authors' rhyme-feature steering (**feature steering**). Outcome: rhyme preference, the log-probability mass on donor rhymes minus mass on original rhymes, at the first token of line 2's last word. Persistence is measured with line 2's words fixed and split by attention path; the paths must add up (additivity gap).

**Reading.** Most of the edit's effect on the rhyme persists when line 2's words are held fixed, so it is not carried by the words the model writes. Of that persistence, direct retrieval of the anchor accounts for about 90-96% at every size; relay is small but above zero. Relay grows in absolute size with the whole effect, but its share rises from 1.7B to 4B and then stays flat. The same-rhyme null shows that the rhyme-preference measure tracks rhyme information rather than the edit's general disruption. A small tail of couplets has a large relay share; it is not yet explained.

## Converging evidence from the authors' released results

Blocking the heads that read the line-1 anchor near the end of line 2 leaves line 2 rhyming in 30% / 20% / 13% / 32% of couplets at 1.7B / 4B / 8B / 14B (all rhymed before): direct retrieval is necessary up to 14B.

## Caveats

- The all-layer state edit is disruptive (with a same-rhyme donor, rhyming falls to 35-51%); behavioural rhyme rates mix steering and disruption. The route split concerns rhyme-specific information and is checked against the feature-steering intervention.
- Rhyme preference counts single-token rhyme words only (Datamuse `rel_rhy`).
- One model family (Qwen3) and one task; 100 couplets per size.
- Unedited generations match the authors' released text exactly on 43-60% of couplets (numerics and generation path); rhyme behaviour is intact.

## Place in the thesis

Supports **retrieval over relay** for the flagship planning example: a plan present at a visible word is looked up where it is needed, not carried. Open: whether relay appears when the information to carry is derived rather than visible (planned derived-value task), whether the relay-heavy tail has an explanation, and the 14B point.
