## 1. What the paper currently claims (plain language)

Imagine you “steer” a small language model by turning up some internal sparse features. The model’s **free** continuation changes from `a pilot` to `an aviator`. It looks as if you found planning features: they affect both the article (`a`/`an`) and the later occupation word.

That observation **does not identify a plan**. In an autoregressive model, once you emit `an` instead of `a`, that new article is now **part of the prompt** for the next word. Vowel-initial words become more likely for ordinary next-token reasons. Call this **generated-token mediation**: the intervention may only change the article; the noun change can be a downstream consequence of the article.

The paper’s contribution is to **measure that ambiguity** with a factorial:

1. Run the intervention off and on.
2. For each, generate freely **and** force the article to `a` or `an`.
3. Keep the intervention **on** during noun prediction even when the article is pasted (so you are not cheating by turning the handle off).
4. Compare three things:
   - **Total effect:** free intervention vs free baseline (big noun change).
   - **Article-only reproduction:** paste the intervention-chosen article **with the intervention off**. Does that already recreate the new noun?
   - **Residual / controlled direct effect:** same pasted article, intervention on vs off. Whatever remains is not explained by the visible article token.

**Result on Gemma 3 270M, pretrained, with Gemma Scope 2 affine features, on 20 held-out occupation prompts:**

- Free generation: 19/20 nouns change; typical twins `pilot→aviator`, `lawyer→attorney`. Almost never “keep the same noun, only change the article.”
- Pasting the intervention-selected article with the intervention **off** recreates the intervention-on **top-1 noun on 20/20 prompts**.
- On the 19 prompts where the article actually switches: total noun-distribution variation ≈ **0.940**, article-only ≈ **0.937**, same-prefix residual ≈ **0.029**.
- A **small nonzero** residual remains on seven pre-specified twins under fixed `an` (about +0.089 logits). So: overwhelmingly mediated, **not** “literally zero direct effect.”
- Sparse features at the next token **do** feel the intervention (propagation is possible). A dense full-residual patch at the article position **can** move nouns a lot (+6.44 logits vs +0.14 random). So the noun assay is not dead; this particular sparse handle just does not carry a comparable independent plan.
- A **synthetic** toy model with known mechanisms shows the same estimands return residual 0 under pure mediation and a large residual under a true persistent plan. That checks the **method**, not Gemma.

**What we do not claim:** latent planning is fake; Hanna & Ameisen (ICLR-style latent planning, 2026) are wrong about scale; Gemma has no content representation anywhere; these two features are “the” a/an circuit of all LMs.

**What we do claim:** for this handle, free-generation noun change is **not identified evidence of persistent latent control**. Generated-token controls are required.

That is the workshop-shaped contribution: an identification standard, with a concrete failure mode of a popular assay.

## 2. Glossary (jargon used in the repo and manuscript)

Read this even if you already know mech-interp. Several terms shifted meaning during the project.

### Models, tools, tokens

- **Token.** Atomic piece of text the model reads or writes. May be a whole word (`Paris`) or a piece (`ophthalm`).
- **Logit.** Unnormalized score for a next token. Differences of logits are **margins** (e.g. \(\ell_{\texttt{an}}-\ell_{\texttt{a}}\)).
- **Autoregression.** After a token is emitted, it becomes input for later tokens. This is the source of generated-token mediation.
- **Gemma 3 270M.** Google’s 270M-parameter Gemma 3 language model. **PT** = pretrained / base (`google/gemma-3-270m`). **IT** = instruction-tuned (`google/gemma-3-270m-it`). Primary paper stack is **PT**.
- **CLT / transcoder / Gemma Scope 2.** A sparse dictionary attached to MLP computations so we can name and intervene on “features.” Cross-layer transcoder (CLT). Affine vs non-affine: **affine** includes a learned skip around the sparse features (better reconstruction; some computation can bypass features). We use PT **`width_262k_l0_medium_affine`**. Feature IDs **do not transfer** across PT/IT or affine/non-affine.
- **Feature ID** such as `L13/F10304`. Layer 13, feature index 10304 in **this** transcoder. Not a human concept label.
- **Circuit Tracer / `circuit-tracer`.** Library that builds attribution graphs and applies feature interventions. Backend **nnsight**. Python: `/Users/anthony/miniconda3/bin/python` (do **not** create a venv). Skills live in `skills/circuit-attribution/`, `skills/circuit-graph-export/`, `skills/circuit-graph-viewer/`.
- **Attribution graph.** Estimated contributions from tokens/features/errors to later features and logits. Suggests candidates; **not** by itself causation.
- **Position \(P\).** Last prompt token / pre-article position: the state used to choose the next token (the article).
- **do(\(x\)).** Interventionist shorthand: force / paste token \(x\) rather than sampling it.
- **Teacher forcing.** Feed gold subsequent tokens so a multi-token word can be scored even if greedy decoding would have left it.

### Interventions

- **LoF / loss-of-function / suppress / zero.** Set feature activation to 0.
- **GoF / gain-of-function / amplify.** Multiply activation (default **\(5\times\)** prompt-specific activation). Can be off-manifold.
- **Rescue.** Undo LoF or apply the opposite GoF and ask whether the original package returns.
- **Activation-matched random control.** Same number of features, similar activation, random identity. Tests “any push breaks the model.”
- **Freeze attention.** Mechanistic ablation: keep attention/normalization from the baseline pass. Primary analyses **recompute** attention.

### Task language (a/an)

- **Article \(b\).** The token `a` or `an`.
- **Noun token \(c\).** First content token of the occupation word (lexical identity, not guaranteed semantic synonymy).
- **Class / onset class.** Consonant-initial vs vowel-initial occupation (phonology of the English article rule).
- **Twin.** Pre-specified near-synonym pair spanning classes, e.g. `pilot`/`aviator`, `lawyer`/`attorney`.
- **Wrapper-like.** Article moves, **same** noun token stays. This is what “edit grammar around a fixed plan” would look like.
- **Trajectory-like / package-like / compiled trajectory / chunking.** Article **and** noun-initial class move together; local grammar stays legal (`a pilot` → `an aviator`). Older notes used “chunking” from neuroscience; the paper now prefers **package** / **trajectory** to avoid over-claiming biology.
- **Illicit mismatch.** Ungrammatical pairing such as `an` + consonant-initial noun. Empirically near zero under our handles.
- **Majority-\(a\) bias.** This 270M model strongly prefers `a`. True ungrammatical `a` + vowel-initial continuations are rare.

### Causal hypotheses (evolved — do not mix eras)

Letters \(a,b,c\) are **tokens**; \(A,B,C\) are putative **internal concepts**.

- **H1 — modular / editable wrapper / latent planning as factorization.** Context builds a content plan \(C\); \(C\) licenses article \(B\); the same \(C\) writes noun \(c\). You should be able to edit the article **without** rewriting the planned noun, or rewrite the noun **with the article held fixed**.
- **H2 — fused package / compiled trajectory.** Article preference and noun class are one causal object. Steering switches legal packages. Independent \(C\to c\) with \(b\) fixed should fail.
- **H3 — article control + autoregressive continuation (generated-token mediation).** The intervention mostly changes \(b\); then \(b\to c\) explains most of the noun change. **No fused package representation is required.** Pasting native \(b\) restores native \(c\).

**Critical later insight:** free-generation package switching is **compatible with both H2 and H3**. Distinguishing them requires the factorial / token-clamped residual. The **manuscript’s main claim is H3 (overwhelming mediation)**, with a small residual, not a knockdown of all H2 structure in the network.

- **TE.** Total free-generation effect on the noun distribution.
- **CDE.** Controlled direct effect: residual noun change with the article (mediator) clamped.
- **TV / JS.** Total variation and Jensen–Shannon distances between next-token distributions. **TV is not an additive “percent mediated.”** Ratios are descriptive.
- **\(k\)-token residual-control curve.** Clamp article plus \(k\) following tokens, remeasure residual. Used in the synthetic check.
- **Forward / backward planning (Latent Planning paper).** Forward: representation causes later content \(t\). Backward: it also shapes earlier licensing context for \(t\). Our paper does not refute their **scale** claim; it tightens **causal identification** when small-model interventions move articles.

## 3. Evolution of the thinking (including abandoned ideas)

This is the journal of how we got to generated-token mediation. A successor who only reads the PDF will miss why we did not start there.

### 3.1 Original practical question: stalling / commitment

**Hope.** Small models emit bridge tokens such as `the` instead of committing to an answer they already “know” (e.g. `Paris`). If we can suppress a reusable stalling circuit, the model should commit earlier. The intervention must **transfer** and be **specific**, not inject `Paris`.

**What we ran.** Country–city prompts (“The city people most strongly associate with France is”). Ranking of `the` vs `Paris` could be flipped, but clean `the`-suppression failed; successful mixes included Paris features; cross-country transfer polluted wrong cities.

**Conclusion.** Negative for a reusable commitment tool. Positive for experimental discipline. **Abandoned as the main paper.** Kept as Appendix-adjacent history / collaborator narrative.

Repo: `experiments/stall_commitment_sprint/`, `cross_country_commitment_screen/`, `shared_stall_suppression/`.

### 3.2 Pivot to Latent Planning’s a/an task

Hanna & Ameisen, *Latent Planning Emerges with Scale* (the ICLR-adjacent paper we followed): a representation **plans** for future content \(t\) only if it causes \(t\) **and** earlier licensing (e.g. `an` before `accountant`). Small models fail behaviorally but may have nascent features. Their main axis was **model scale**. We cannot sweep scale on a 16 GB M1. We took their **task + definition** on **one small model**.

**Behavior.** Gemma 270M strongly prefers `a`. True mismatches (`a ophthalmologist`) are rare; the model usually stays grammatical by choosing a consonant-initial noun.

**Discovery case.** Prompt: “Someone who treats eye diseases is” (with an `a`-demonstration). Baseline: `a ophthalmologist`. Future-`ophthalm` information is active before the article. Competing-pathway screen: suppress features that favor `a` over `an` with low direct effect on `ophthalm`. Singles did not flip. Pair **`L13/F10304` + `L14/F1949`** (suppress at final prompt token) produced `an ophthalmologist` with the noun preserved **on that prompt**.

**Selection bias (later recognized).** We searched for the signature a wrapper story predicts. We did **not** systematically amplify `an`/content features, mix suppress+amplify, or nominate features by recurrence across prompts (that *was* tried in stalling, not for this pair).

Repo: `ophthalmologist_planning_pilot/`, `ophthalmologist_competing_pathway_screen/`.

### 3.3 Held-out collapse: not grammar repair

The same frozen pair on 107 held-outs: some expected-`an` “repairs,” many expected-`a` controls flipped to `an`, first content word changed on 37/107, **zero** grammar-only content-preserving repairs. `a pilot` → `an aviator`, etc.

**Interpretation shift.** Not anti-stalling, not content-preserving planning repair. Coordinated **response classes**.

**Framing trap we avoided:** hanging the paper on “Gemma often plans \(t\) but emits the wrong article.” Mismatches are rare. The robust object is class switching on **already grammatical** prompts. Ophthalmologist is discovery, not the population.

Repo: `fixed_pair_generalization/`.

### 3.4 Ideas considered and dropped or demoted

- **“Prove small models chunk but habit gating fails.”** Attractive neuroscience rhetoric (Miller / Graybiel). **Dropped as unique claim.** Chunking is a communication lens; the other-agent “suppress habit → same chunk executes” prediction is exactly what held-out **failed**. Novelty is **modular plan vs compiled trajectory vs article-mediated continuation**, not “LLMs also chunk.”
- **Scale sweep; Qwen 3 family; couplet poetry.** Hardware and scope. **Out.**
- **Second task `is`/`are`.** Screen found no usable mismatches. **Not a main-paper bet.**
- **Switch to IT to match Latent Planning’s main figures.** Briefly implemented (IT smoke graph). Hub IT `medium_affine` **layer 0 is empty**. Non-affine IT is a weaker a/an instrument (their Appendix J). **Abandoned as primary stack.** Stay PT+affine. Do not mix PT model with IT transcoder.
- **524k affine IT dictionary.** Better toolkit, ~2× memory. **Not needed** once PT affine worked.
- **Neuroscience mapping / basal ganglia.** Too soft for NeurIPS. Optional vocabulary only.
- **Content-preserving wrapper as the existence proof we must find.** We looked (E1–E4, force-native, fixed-\(b\) assays). We did **not** find a clean held-out wrapper. The paper is stronger as an **identification** result than as “we discovered packages.”
- **Claiming H2 (fused packages) from free generation alone.** Later rejected as under-identified. Free `pilot→aviator` is also what H3 predicts.
- **MLP-in “oracle” at the pre-article position as positive control.** Failed to move nouns. Replaced by a **full-residual patch at the article position** (`fixed_article_residual_reference/`).
- **Living file `experiments/PAPER_EXPERIMENTS.md` as pipeline source of truth.** Frozen 7 August 2026 as the E1–E6 contract. **Now marked historical.** Live claim is `paper.qmd`.

### 3.5 Stricter causality, then the clincher

Biology-style language entered: necessity / sufficiency / rescue / specificity / content preservation. Latent Planning’s bar (per-example “planning features,” article accuracy, random controls) is **above** observational probing and **below** a stable-factor biology bar. Room to challenge **evidential sufficiency**, not to pretend they did no causal work.

**Clincher (8 August 2026 era):** Latent-Planning-style **gain-of-function** on frozen recurring dual-effect features, scored by **content identity**, not article alone. Result: huge \(\Delta(\texttt{an}-\texttt{a})\) vs controls, content preserved ~5%, class shift ~95%. `a pilot` → `an aviator` (already grammatical; nothing to “fix”).

Repo: `planning_gain_content_clincher/`.

### 3.6 E1–E6: no wrapper window

Execution contract: `experiments/PAPER_EXPERIMENTS.md` (historical). Results (PT affine):

- **E1** selection rules: only S1 is strongly trajectory-like; **no** rule is wrapper-like on held-out.
- **E2** dose: package-like rate rises with gain; wrapper stays ~0.
- **E3** dual lock (article-push + content-lock): does not unlock content-preserving article repair; illicit mismatch ≈ 0. This was supposed to be the A/B clincher for modular vs compiled.
- **E4** tetrad on twins: GoF moves packages above controls.
- **E5** slim non-occupation transfer: still moves more than controls (local-ish).
- **E6** ophthalmologist still looks wrapper-like **on source** — consistent with “source looks modular, transfer does not.”

Then still more H1-stress tests: selective intervention only at the article step then paste native article (`selective_bc_force_native/` — native \(c\) returns); fixed-\(b\) within-class noun moves with content clamps **on** at \(c\) (null); per-noun LP-style features (null); dense residual direction (null). Collaborator report Stages XV–XVIII.

**Those nulls are real and still in the appendix/collaborator narrative.** They support “we could not independently rewrite \(c\) with \(b\) fixed.” They do **not** by themselves prove a fused package **representation** (H2), because H3 also predicts those nulls if the sparse handle is mostly an article dial.

### 3.7 The identification pivot (the current paper)

External review (including ChatGPT Pro as a critic) and our own `b→c` discussion forced the factorial. Commit `281fda7` (11 August 2026) reframed the manuscript around **generated-token mediation**. Commit `3a95e1f` (25 August 2026) added the **synthetic estimand check** so a small residual cannot be dismissed as “the metric always says mediation.”

**H1 / H2 / H3 in the PDF:**

- H1 would need content-preserving article edits or independent \(c\) control under clamped \(b\) of comparable magnitude to the free effect. Not observed for S1.
- H2 remains a possible *internal* ontology, but **output coordination does not identify it**.
- H3 is what the factorial **positively** measures: article insertion recreates the noun.

The collaborator report’s section 10.15 still leans on **H2 as the working hypothesis**. That document is a rich history; it is **slightly behind** the manuscript. If they conflict, **the PDF / `manuscript/sections/` win.**

## 8. One-paragraph briefing you can paste

We study whether Latent-Planning-style sparse interventions on a small LM identify a persistent plan for a later word. On pretrained Gemma 3 270M, amplifying a frozen dual-effect feature set reliably switches free completions (`a pilot` → `an aviator`). An exact intervention-by-article factorial shows that inserting the intervention-chosen article with the intervention off recreates the new noun on 20/20 held-out prompts; the same-prefix residual is small but nonzero. A synthetic ground-truth pair confirms the estimands can detect true residual control when it exists. The paper’s claim is methodological and causal: generated-token mediation must be measured before treating later-token change as latent planning.
